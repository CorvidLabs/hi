What we already have, honestly

spec-sync occupies more of this territory than it first appears, and any design that ignores that ships a second CorvidLabs acceptance-criteria format into the same repos.

It already has: requirements.md companions with a literal ## Acceptance Criteria heading; REQ-<module>-<number> IDs validated to contain SHALL; and a ChangeRecord.acceptance_criteria field on the change lifecycle, settable with specsync change answer <id> acceptance_criteria "<text>". The change document itself already has an ## Intent section.

So "spec-sync owns the contract, hi owns the intent" is only half true. What those criteria genuinely lack is identity. A criterion is an untyped string in one change's ledger, checked for nothing but non-emptiness. It cannot be referenced, cannot be counted, cannot be marked met, and does not survive the change that created it.

The second problem is drift in the other direction. Left inside a spec, acceptance criteria stop describing what a person wanted:

"Discovery retains at most 10,000 spec snapshots, at most 4 MiB per spec, and at most 64 MiB of spec bytes cumulatively."

"The retained project capability used to open the specs directory is also the sole authority for mapped-source snapshots, so replacing the ambient project-root name cannot mix different project identities in one validation result."

spec-sync/specs/cmd_issues/requirements.md, under "## Acceptance Criteria"

Both lines are correct and both belong in a spec. Neither is something a human ever said they wanted. That gap between what was asked for and what got written down is the whole reason hi exists.

The evidence

Syntax is not the problem

Before picking a grammar, look at what happens when AI tooling actually ships one. We classified 291 acceptance criteria pulled from seven public repositories using Kiro-style spec-driven requirements.md files (the largest deployment of EARS-for-agents in the wild).

Almost none of it is EARS.

37.5%

contain and / or (compound criteria that can half-pass)

33.7%

are passive-voice, with no named actor

8.6%

use vague pronouns with no clear referent

The conclusion that should drive every other decision: a format that enforces sentence shape gets gamed in four lines out of five, while nine in ten criteria stay unverifiable. Whatever hi checks first, it must not be keyword conformance.

The survey

Seven formats, and what each one is good for

EARS

Mavin et al., Rolls-Royce, 2009 · Airbus, NASA, Bosch, Intel, Siemens

Borrow the shapes 
# Five patterns and one combinator. The whole grammar.

ubiquitous  The <system> shall <response>.
state       While <precondition>, the <system> shall <response>.
event       When <trigger>, the <system> shall <response>.
optional    Where <feature>, the <system> shall <response>.
unwanted    If <trigger>, then the <system> shall <response>.
complex     While <precondition>, when <trigger>, the <system> shall <response>.

// "While there is no card in the ATM, the ATM shall
//  display 'insert card to begin'."

Works

Deterministically parseable: zero-or-many preconditions, at most one trigger, exactly one system, one-or-more responses. Seventeen years of safety-critical use. It covers rule-shaped and scenario-shaped criteria in one grammar, which no other format does.

Breaks

Unwieldy past three preconditions, and its own authors say it is wrong for anything better expressed as a table, a formula, or a state machine. And as measured above, conformance is trivially faked.

For hiTake the five shapes as suggestions the linter can recognise and offer, never as a gate. A criterion that reads naturally and names a measurable outcome beats one that matches a template and doesn't.

Volere fit criterion

Robertson & Robertson · the "snow card"

Adopt 
Description   The product shall identify all outstanding
              books on loan to a Borrower.

Rationale     Need to know which loans are overdue when
              deciding whether to extend one.

Fit Criterion The Outstanding Book Loans for a Borrower are
              those where the Loan Expiry Date is before
              or equal to Today's Date.

Works

This is the primitive everyone else is missing. The requirement sentence stays human and intent-shaped; a separate field carries the machine-checkable predicate. Their rule is the sharpest test in the whole field: if you cannot write a fit criterion, the requirement is ambiguous or not yet understood.

Breaks

The full snow card has thirteen fields. Nobody fills in thirteen fields. Taken whole it is exactly the ceremony that gets abandoned in week three.

For hiTake three fields, not thirteen: the human sentence, the fit criterion, and how it gets verified. This is the single highest-value borrowing in this document.

RFC 2119 / 8174

IETF BCP 14 · MUST / SHOULD / MAY

Adopt 
MUST    absolute requirement
SHOULD  valid reasons may exist to ignore this, but the
        full implications must be understood and weighed
MAY     truly optional

# RFC 8174: the words carry these meanings ONLY when
# capitalised. Lowercase "should" is just English.

Works

Gives a clean answer to "is this criterion blocking?", which CI needs. The capitalisation rule also kills a measured class of false positives. One study found 16 of 19 and 29 of 37 "loophole" lint findings came from flagging the modal should.

Breaks

Reading as legalese if it lands in the sentence. RFC 2119 itself warns the keywords "MUST only be used where it is actually required"; sprinkling SHALL over everything is how spec prose starts sounding inhuman.

For hiMake strength an explicit field with three values, not a word buried in the prose. The human writes "it has to"; the file records must.

Gherkin / Given-When-Then

Cucumber, SpecFlow, Reqnroll · 20 years of BDD

Borrow the shape only 
Scenario: A registered user logs in
  Given I am on the sign-in screen
  When "Bob" logs in
  Then I land on my own timeline

# The grammar is fine. The mandatory step-definition
# layer underneath it is what kills projects.

Works

Concrete examples are the best cure for ambiguity ever found, and Given-When-Then is the most widely understood way to write one. Tooling exists in every language.

Breaks

Every documented failure traces to the glue code, not the grammar: step-definition explosion, a global step namespace where expressions silently collide, shared World state causing flakes, and stakeholders who stop reading feature files after two sprints. Tricentis killed SpecFlow on 2024-12-31; SmartBear handed Cucumber to the Open Source Collective.

For hiExamples attach to a criterion as illustrations of its fit criterion: optional, additive, never a parallel artifact with its own maintenance cost. A criterion is never required to have executable glue to be valid.

OpenFastTrace

Open-source requirement tracing · markdown-native

Adopt the ID model 
### Markdown Import
`feat~markdown-import~1`        ← type ~ name ~ revision

OFT imports specification items from Markdown files.

Needs: req

# Coverage is claimed from the source file itself:
// [impl->dsn~validate-authentication-request~1]

# Bump the revision when the meaning changes, and every
# downstream claim that still points at ~1 breaks loudly.

Works

The ~revision suffix is the best answer anyone has to rot: intent changes, the revision bumps, and every stale coverage claim surfaces immediately instead of silently lying. Coverage tags work across 30+ languages, plus markdown and Gherkin. Its stated posture is explicitly anti-compliance-theatre.

Breaks

It has no concept of intent, of a fit criterion, or of whether a criterion is any good. It traces links; it does not judge content.

For hiTake the identity model wholesale. That means opaque IDs, never reused, with a revision that invalidates downstream claims. Take the status vocabulary too. Spend hi's own effort on the layer OFT deliberately doesn't have.

Kiro requirements.md

AWS · EARS-flavoured, agent-generated

Avoid the ID scheme 
### Requirement 2
User Story: As a reader, I want <goal>

Acceptance Criteria
1. WHEN the user starts the quiz THEN the system SHALL
   present exactly 9 questions: 3 easy, 3 medium, 3 hard
2. WHEN targeting the audience THEN the system SHALL use
   engaging, discovery-focused language

# Referenced elsewhere as: _Requirements: 1.1, 2.3_
# Insert a requirement above and every one of those
# references silently points somewhere else.

Works

The three-file split (requirements, design, tasks) is a genuinely good separation, and it put spec-driven development in front of a large audience. Criterion 1 above is exactly right: a number, a breakdown, no wiggle room.

Breaks

IDs are positional. 1.1 means "first criterion of the first requirement", so inserting anything renumbers everything below it and every back-reference repoints with no error. Criterion 2 above shows the other half: well-formed, and impossible to fail.

For hiThis is the format to define ourselves against. Positional IDs are the specific mistake hi's permanent, append-only identity exists to avoid.

GitHub spec-kit

Constitution → spec → plan → tasks

Borrow two primitives 
**FR-006**: System MUST authenticate users via
  [NEEDS CLARIFICATION: auth method not specified.
   Email/password, SSO, OAuth?]

**SC-001**: Users can complete account creation in
  under 2 minutes

# Review markers: [Gap] [Ambiguity] [Conflict] [Assumption]

Works

[NEEDS CLARIFICATION] is the best idea in the whole field: it turns "the human hasn't decided yet" from invisible prose into a countable, blocking state. Its review framing, "test the requirements, not the implementation", is the right instinct.

Breaks

Volume. The loudest recurring complaint is verbatim: "spec-kit created a LOT of markdown files for me to review. They were repetitive... tedious to review." and "I'd rather review code than all these markdown files."

For hiTake the clarification marker and the [Gap] [Ambiguity] [Conflict] [Assumption] vocabulary. Ship one small file, never a directory tree.

The proposal

What hi should be

Human lines, numbered by feature, with permanent identifiers. You can dictate the left-hand side of this file and never think about the format at all.

# hi · corvid-chat

## Intent
I want to talk to people I trust without anyone in the middle
being able to read it, and without it feeling like a security
product. It should feel like texting.

## A · Send a message to someone I already trust

A.1  I type a message and hit enter → it shows up in the thread
     right away, marked as sending.
     fit: visible in thread before any network round-trip
     by:  test ChatThread.optimisticAppend

A.2  It reaches them → the mark changes to sent. I never have
     to wonder whether it went.
     fit: delivery receipt flips state within 5s on a live link
     by:  test Delivery.receiptFlipsState

A.3  If I have no connection → it queues and tells me so.
     It does not silently disappear.
     fit: queued count visible; zero dropped across 100 sends
     by:  test Outbox.survivesOffline

A.4  [NEEDS CLARIFICATION: what happens to a queued message
     if I delete the thread before it sends?]

## B · Add someone new

B.1  I paste in their code → they show up in my list.
     fit: contact appears without app restart
     by:  test Contacts.addByCode

Everything to the left of fit: is what a person says out loud. Everything after it is what makes the line checkable, and it is only required when you claim the criterion is met, never when you write it down.

The rules that make it work

  1. Permanent IDs, append-only. A.3 is a name, not a position. New criteria append to the end of their group and never renumber anything. A retired criterion is marked retired and keeps its ID forever; the number is never reused.
  2. Revisions invalidate claims. When the meaning of a criterion changes, it becomes A.3~2 and every piece of evidence still pointing at ~1 is reported stale. This is OpenFastTrace's mechanism, and it is the only proven answer to intent quietly rotting away from code.
  3. Capture is free, claiming is not. You can write (or dictate) a bare line with no fit: at all and it is a valid criterion in draft. It cannot move to met without one. This is the whole design: zero friction at the moment of thinking, real rigour at the moment of claiming done.
  4. Two lint tiers, two exit codes. Structural problems fail: unparseable line, claimed-met with no fit criterion, evidence pointing at a revision that no longer exists. Style smells only warn: vague adjectives, compound and/or, passive voice, missing actor. Smell detection measures roughly 59% precision at 82% recall, and a two-in-five false-alarm rate on a blocking gate is how a tool gets disabled in a week.
  5. Suppression is first-class. Any warning can be rejected inline and stays rejected, and any rule can be turned off per project. Copy the spell-checker model exactly.
  6. One file. Not a directory tree. The loudest complaint about every tool in this space is the volume of markdown it generates for humans to review.

What comes out of it

The file is the source; tickets and specs are generated views of it, which is what makes the human-first ordering pay for itself.

  • Tickets. A criterion is already the right size for an issue. Its ID is the permanent link back, so a closed ticket can be traced to the exact line of intent it satisfied, and a reopened criterion can find its old ticket.
  • Specs. A group of criteria becomes the seed for a spec-sync module: ## A maps to a requirement, its fit: lines map to acceptance criteria, and fledge spec new takes it from there. hi writes the why; spec-sync owns the contract.
  • Evidence. by: is a binding, not prose. It is a test id, a command with an expected exit code, or an explicit human sign-off. That is the field that separates a criterion from a wish.

And because the human half of the file is just sentences, dictation works without any adaptation. Local speech-to-text with an LLM cleanup pass is a solved problem, so this is a prompt rather than a feature.

Before we build

Two things to settle

The name has a collision. hi on crates.io is taken and actively maintained (v0.1.18, ~21k downloads), but it is library-only with no binaries, so the hi command is free. Publish the crate as hi-cli with [[bin]] name = "hi", and always write it as "hi (Human Intent)" in anything searchable. Homebrew core has no hi formula.

Don't claim to be first. PoHI, FITPAC, codespec and agent-spec all make adjacent claims. The defensible claim is narrower and fully verifiable: no acceptance-criteria format in this space has permanent criterion identity and a machine-resolvable verification binding, and no two of them can exchange a criterion without a human retyping it.

  • Does fit: stay optional at capture time, or does hi prompt for it immediately while the thought is fresh?
  • Standalone binary, fledge plugin, or a binary that fledge wraps?
  • Does hi write its own file, or add criterion IDs to the ## Acceptance Criteria section spec-sync already has?
